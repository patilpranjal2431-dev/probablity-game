1. Exact Dice Sums via Dynamic Programming (app/quant/exact.py)

Plain idea: If you roll 5 six-sided dice, listing every sequence takes 6^5 = 7,776 steps (and for 15 dice, over 470 billion!). Instead, dynamic programming builds the distribution one die at a time.

  • Start with 1 way to reach sum 0: {0: 1}.
  • For each new die, walk through your current (sum, ways) table and add each face 1..6 to get the next table.
  • Divide each final count by sides^num_dice for exact probabilities.

2. Counting All 2,598,960 Five-Card Poker Hands (app/quant/exact.py)

Plain idea: Every 5-card hand from a 52-card deck belongs to exactly one of 10 categories. Using combinations C(n, k), we count each tier while carefully subtracting overlaps so no hand is counted twice:

  • Royal Flush: 4 hands (10-J-Q-K-A in any of the 4 suits).
  • Straight Flush: 10 × 4 − 4 = 36 (10 possible high cards from 5-high wheel to A-high, minus the 4 royals).
  • Flush (non-straight): 4 × C(13, 5) − 40 = 5,108.
  • High Card: (C(13, 5) − 10) × (4^5 − 4) = 1,302,540 (non-straight ranks with non-monochrome suits).

All 10 tiers sum to 2,598,960, verified automatically by pytest. The same module also computes Hypergeometric probabilities (drawing without replacement) and the Birthday Collision curve (50.73% at 23 people).

3. Monte Carlo, Wilson Confidence Intervals & 1/√n Convergence (app/quant/monte_carlo.py, convergence.py)

Plain idea: Simulate n independent trials with a fixed seed (so runs are 100% reproducible) and count the fraction of hits p_hat = successes / n.

  • Why Wilson instead of the naive Wald interval? When p is near 0 or 1, the textbook formula p_hat ± 1.96 √(p_hat(1-p_hat)/n) can collapse to zero width or leave [0, 1]. The Wilson score interval solves the confidence quadratic directly so bounds stay strictly in [0, 1] and maintain ~95% coverage.
  • Why error shrinks as 1/√n: Standard deviation drops with the square root of sample size. To cut Monte Carlo error in half, you need 4× as many simulations, producing a -0.5 slope on a log-log error chart.

4. The Four Quantitative Bots (app/games/bots.py)

Plain idea: Every bot receives the exact same privacy-safe PlayerObservation (its own card/die, pot size, legal actions, and exact showdown equity) and plays through the pure-Python state machine:

  • Random Bot: Picks uniformly from legal moves and bet sizes. Acts as the sanity baseline.
  • Cautious Bot: Demands a +15% equity buffer above pot odds to call and only value-bets the minimum with ≥ 80% equity.
  • EV-Maximising Bot: Risk-neutral. Calls whenever equity × (pot + to_call) − to_call ≥ 0, and when first to act picks the bet size (or check) with highest expected coin gain against a pot-odds opponent.
  • Kelly-Sizing Bot: Maximizes long-run logarithmic stack growth using half-Kelly sizing (0.5 × edge × own_stack), scaling down bets to avoid busting.

5. Decision EV Grading & Regret (app/games/grading.py)

Plain idea: Winning a hand with bad math is luck; losing a hand after making the right call is good skill hit by variance. After each live hand finishes, Edge Lab replays your exact decision moment (before your opponent's card/die was revealed) and grades every legal option:

Regret = Best Legal EV − Taken EV ≥ 0

Your cumulative EV powers the Skill Leaderboard, tracked separately from the Coins Leaderboard (Skill + Luck).

6. Bankroll Simulator: Kelly Criterion & Risk of Ruin (app/quant/bankroll.py)

Plain idea: Even a favorable bet can wipe you out if you bet too big. For win probability p and net payout odds b:

  • Full Kelly Fraction: f* = max(0, p − (1 − p) / b) maximizes long-run compound growth E[ln(B_{t+1} / B_t)]. Overbetting past 2f* turns positive edge into negative compound growth.
  • Gambler's Ruin: For fixed even-money bets (b = 1) starting with k units, the exact chance of ever hitting zero is ((1 − p) / p)^k when p > 0.5.

7. Probability Calibration & Brier Score (app/quant/calibration.py)

Plain idea: If you say an event has a 70% chance, it should happen roughly 7 out of 10 times. Edge Lab generates random dice, poker, hypergeometric, and birthday prompts with a secret server outcome y ∈ {0, 1} and scores your forecast p_hat using:

Brier Score = (p_hat − y)² ∈ [0, 1]   (0 = perfect, 0.25 = coin-flip 50% guess)

Because Brier score is a strictly proper scoring rule, you minimize your expected score only by reporting your honest probability belief—hedging toward 50% or overconfident 0%/100% predictions both hurt your long-run score.

8. Order Book Market Making & Adverse Selection (app/games/order_book.py)

Plain idea: A market maker posts both a buy price (bid) and a sell price (ask) around an unobserved fair value V_t, seeing only a noisy public signal.

  • Noise & Retail Bots trade unpredictably and pay you the half-spread (ask − bid)/2.
  • Informed Arbitrage Bots know V_t and immediately snipe your quote whenever your ask is too low or your bid is too high (adverse selection).
  • Exact P&L Conservation Identity: After the session ends, Edge Lab reveals V_t and verifies down to the cent that: Total P&L = Spread Captured − Adverse Selection Cost + Inventory Drift P&L.

9. Cryptographic RNG & Append-Only Coin Ledger (app/games/rng.py, app/ledger.py)

  • Dual RNG Interface (GameRNG): Live games, WebSocket tables, calibration prompts, and order books use LiveRNG (random.SystemRandom backed by OS cryptographic entropy). Monte Carlo labs and bot tournaments use SeededRNG so any experiment can be reproduced identically from its seed.
  • Append-Only Ledger: Every account starts with 1,000 virtual coins. Wallets are never edited arbitrarily—every stake, win, refund, or forfeiture writes an immutable Transaction row with balance_after, and audit_wallet_balance() verifies that the full history sums exactly to the current balance.
